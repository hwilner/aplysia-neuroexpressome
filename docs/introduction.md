# AplysiaNeuroExpress: a corrected neuron gene-model set with a curated ion-channel and plasticity-gene catalogue

## Introduction

### The annotation bottleneck in a model organism built on identified neurons

*Aplysia californica* occupies an unusual position in experimental neuroscience. Its neurons are individually identifiable, uniquely named, and re-identifiable between preparations — a property that let Susswein and Byrne map the neurons that initiate patterned buccal activity in 1988 [Susswein1988], and that has supported three decades of work on which cells decide whether an animal bites, swallows, or rejects. That same property is what makes molecular work on *Aplysia* unusually tractable: the bag cell neurons yield nearly two micrograms of total RNA from a single isolated soma [Moroz2006], and identified neurons can be dissected by eye and pipetted individually for transcriptomic analysis.

The difficulty is that the molecular infrastructure has not kept pace with the cellular biology. The reference assembly, AplCal3.0, was released in 2013 as a scaffold-level assembly of roughly 927 Mbp distributed across 17 haploid chromosomes [Narins2026AplysiaResource], and the automated NCBI annotation built on top of it is RefSeq Annotation Release 102, dated 2020 [Narins2026AplysiaResource]. Where Susswein's physiology describes a circuit cell by cell, the genome describes genes by fragment. The animal's central neurons are also highly polyploid [NeurobaseAplysia], which decouples transcriptional output from gene copy number in exactly the cells where gene dosage effects are most often invoked.

The consequence is visible in the methods sections of recent papers. Between 2023 and 2025, groups working on *Aplysia* neuropeptides and receptors reported searching *both* the RefSeq genome annotation *and* the separate 2022 CNS transcriptome assembly hosted at AplysiaTools, because neither was sufficient on its own and full-length sequences had to be recovered by hand [Chen2023Allatostatin; Chen2023Elevenin; Zhang2025CellRep; Sossin2019Kurodai]. The elevenin-receptor study put it plainly: the three *Aplysia* mRNA entries in NCBI were incomplete, and complete sequences had to be found elsewhere [Chen2023Elevenin]. This is not a criticism of any individual group. It is a structural gap, and structural gaps are what shared resources exist to close.

### Why the underlying methods are ready and simply have not been applied here

The tooling needed to repair this gap is not hypothetical. Hsiao and colleagues published a complete, reproducible pipeline in 2024 for using long-read single-molecule RNA-seq to repair genome gene models and improve phylogenomic inference, demonstrating gene-model replacement by transcriptome alignment, family-based tree inspection, and selective removal of partial, expanded, or broken models in favour of better-supported transcriptome sequences [Hsiao2024OceanToTree]. Independently, a 2025 benchmarking study of twelve annotation pipelines across the tree of life found that TOGA, BRAKER3, and StringTie consistently topped the field on BUSCO recovery, coding-sequence length, and false-positive rate [Gabriel2025Annotations]. Meanwhile the closest gastropod relatives have had exactly this treatment: the sacoglossan sea slug *Elysia timida* received a chromosome-level assembly annotated with BRAKER 3.0.8, yielding 19,904 protein-coding genes contiguous across metazoan BUSCOs [Martinez2025Elysia], and the bobtail squid *Euprymna scolopes* received a BRAKER2 annotation of 18,663 gene models [Rogers2025Euprymna].

So the method is established, the gastropod precedent is two years old, and the *Aplysia* reference annotation is six. The gap is not technical difficulty. It is that the genome is owned by a specific group (University of Florida, the Broad Institute, and Columbia's Kandel and Ju laboratories, working through a hybrid Illumina/PacBio/Sanger history [NeurobaseAplysia]) and that group has its own long-read programme.

That is precisely why this project must be scoped to the curation layer rather than the assembly layer. Nobody benefits from a fourth partial re-annotation of a dataset they can see the raw reads for. A channel-family catalogue that reconciles scattered gene fragments against a coherent model set, states confidence per locus, and is directly queryable by people doing electrophysiology, is a different artifact with a different user base.

### Why ion channels are the right payload

Ion channels are the correct payload for a curation project in this organism for three reasons.

First, the family is tractable. The *Aplysia* neuronal transcriptome carries on the order of 14 to 16 potassium channels, 8 voltage-gated calcium channels, 2 voltage-gated sodium channels, 12 ionotropic glutamate receptors, and 16 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor transcripts [Moroz2006]. That is a curated-scale list, small enough for genuine manual review rather than automated pass-through, and large enough to matter mechanistically.

Second, isoform structure is known to be functionally decisive. Detailed work on *ApCaV2*, the calcium channel required for transmitter release in sensory neurons, resolved 49 coding-region exons and 5 untranslated-region exons and enumerated the eight previously characterised alternative splicing sites, yielding an estimate of more than 700 possible *ApCaV2a1* isoforms from combinations of alternative start sites, splice sites, and exon usage [Wang2023CaV2]. A resource that reports *Aplysia* channel loci at gene-model level only would be inadequate to the isoform biology already established.

Third, the channel-to-behaviour link in this organism is a documented research programme rather than an inference. Bédécarrats, Simmers, and Nargeot established that the B63 decision interneuron's pacemaker rhythm arises from organelle-derived intracellular calcium flux acting on voltage-insensitive membrane channels, and specifically excluded a contribution from voltage-dependent channels by clamping membrane potential over the relevant range [Bedecarrats2021]. Building on that, the same group showed B63's bursting plateau potentials require voltage-dependent sodium inward currents, with calcium-regulated potassium outward current contributing to active termination [Bedecarrats2023]. A curated channel catalogue is what makes such claims checkable against genome-level reality: one can ask, without leaving the database, which channel models exist, how fragmented they are, and which isoform structures are recoverable.

### The connection to learning that food is inedible

This project is a methods-and-resource study, and it would be dishonest to disguise it as physiology. But its scientific payload lands in a specific and still-open place.

Susswein's laboratory established that memory for inedible food requires three contingent events during training, and that attempts to swallow are signalled by histamine and nitric oxide [Katzoff2002J Neurosci; Katzoff2006JNeurophysiol; Katzoff2010LearnMem]. NO signalling is not a permissive background condition: inhibiting NO synthesis before training blocks short-term, intermediate-term, and long-term memory alike [Katzoff2002J Neurosci]. The group then localised the transcriptional response, finding that training with inedible food increased *C/EBP* mRNA in the buccal ganglia — ganglia with a primarily motor function, an unusual site for a taste-specific learning signal — and not in the cerebral or pleural ganglia, with a roughly 11.9-fold increase over naive controls [Levitan2008Cebp]. A later dissection of the components showed that brief training and an NO donor each independently increased *C/EBP*, *CREB1*, *CREB1alpha*, and *CREB1beta* in the buccal ganglia, with the NO donor additionally affecting cerebral expression, indicating that long-term memory arises through both additive and interactive increases in gene expression [BriskinLuchinsky2018].

Critically, the molecular signals are not confined to a single cell class. Levitan and colleagues localised molecular correlates of memory consolidation to the buccal ganglia mechanoafferent neurons themselves [Levitan2012Localization], and Hurwitz, Tam, Jing, Chiel, Gill, and Susswein showed in 2024 that learning differentially changes the fast synaptic connections from the S1 mechanoafferent cluster to each of five distinct followers, with unique patterns of change per follower and, in one case, differences between regions of the S1 cluster [Hurwitz2024Connectivity; Hurwitz2024Repeated]. The behavioural correlate is set by exactly the circuit that the buccal transcriptome serves.

The unresolved step is identification. The group's stated next step after demonstrating that protein synthesis during sleep blocks consolidation of new memories was to identify the memory-blocking proteins and determine how they prevent memory formation [Levy2016eLife; SussweinLab2016]. Doing that in the buccal ganglia requires a correct gene model for the buccal transcriptome. A resource that makes ion channel and plasticity-gene loci reliable in that ganglion is a direct enabling step for that programme, and it is a step that does not require the group to stop what it is doing.

### What this project delivers, and what it does not

The deliverable is a versioned, publicly archived set of neuron-focused gene models for *A. californica* built by lifting the 2022 CNS transcriptome onto AplCal3.0 and repairing the resulting models, together with a hand-curated, confidence-scored catalogue of ion channel and plasticity-related loci carrying domain assignments, membrane topology, isoform structure, and orthologs.

It is explicitly not a new assembly, not a claim of completeness, and not a replacement for the genome-owning group's own annotation effort. The manuscript's value proposition is a working artefact plus a measured improvement in gene-model integrity for a defined gene set, with every curation decision auditable.
